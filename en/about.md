I am Paul Sauvé. I am an agricultural producer in Shefford, in Quebec's Eastern Townships, and the founder of GNR Shefford. I did not set out to challenge a government, to retain litigators, or to spend years of my life and a large part of my own resources defending a project that was approved, contracted, and built. That is simply where the system left me.

## What I built

GNR Shefford is a biomethanization project on farmland at 36 chemin Bell. Its purpose is straightforward. It takes agricultural and organic residues and turns them into renewable natural gas that can be injected into Quebec's gas network, in place of the fossil shale gas the province imports every day. It is exactly the kind of local, agricultural, renewable energy that Quebec's own programs were created to support.

The project is not a concept or a promise. It holds the required authorizations. It has a signed, long-term offtake contract. It secured private construction financing. And it qualified for support under a Quebec government program designed precisely for projects like this one.

## What happened

Under a signed agreement, the Convention PSPGNR V2004, the Quebec government committed more than 10.8 million dollars to the project. A first portion, roughly 2.7 million dollars, was paid. The remainder was withheld.

When the committed funds did not arrive, the matter went before the Quebec Superior Court. On 13 August 2025, the Court ordered disbursement (docket 500-17-134848-251). The obligation is now a matter of public record, confirmed by a judge. And still the project waits.

## What it has cost

This is the part that does not appear in any budget line.

To pursue funds the government had committed, a private citizen has to hire lawyers, prepare filings, and carry the legal costs of standing against an institution with far greater resources and time. A farmer has to do this while still running a farm, while interest accrues, while a construction lender's deadline draws closer, and while the project that was meant to produce revenue instead produces debt.

It is years of work. It is sleepless nights. It is the steady, grinding pressure of being told, in effect, to wait, to file again, to provide one more thing, while the clock runs against you and the cost of every delay lands entirely on you.

I am not writing this for sympathy. I am writing it because this is what accountability costs an ordinary person in Quebec when the system decides to stop, and because no one should have to go to court to receive money a government committed by contract and was ordered by a judge to pay.

## What I am actually asking

The question is not why I was treated this way. The question is simpler, and it is the only one that matters.

Where is the money that was committed, announced, signed for, and ordered paid?

That question has a documented answer in the public record. It is why I have chosen to lay that record out plainly and in full, rather than to argue about motives or personalities. The facts are enough.

## About this work

Everything published under my name traces back to public documents: court filings, regulatory records, signed agreements, and official program rules. The aim is not to accuse. It is to document, to make a complex public-finance story understandable, and to ask the questions the public record makes unavoidable.

**This record will continue to be documented as the matter proceeds.**

Paul Sauvé  
Agricultural producer, founder of GNR Shefford
